Math · Calculus

Limits → derivatives → chain rule → gradient: the math of “which way to move each parameter”.

The calculus used to train neural networks, with a step-by-step proof for each result; formulas are rendered locally with no external service.

Why it’s designed this way: Memorised formulas fade; following the proof tells you when a result holds and when it does not.

▶ Open the interactive version (tap the diagram, try labs, take the quiz)

🧒 In plain words

Training an AI is like turning thousands of little knobs on a radio at once, trying to make the “mistakes” (the loss) smaller and smaller. But which way should each knob turn?

The derivative is the answer: it says “if this knob moves a tiny bit, how much do the mistakes change?”, like telling you which way the ground slopes under your feet and how steep it is. Walk downhill and the mistakes shrink.

Step by step, this stop covers: limits (getting closer and closer) → derivatives (how steep) → the chain rule (links in a chain) → gradients (many knobs at once).
