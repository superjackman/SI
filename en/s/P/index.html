Probability basics

Distributions, conditionals, mean/variance, Gaussians

▶ Open the interactive version (diagrams, labs, quizzes)

To predict the next token, we must speak in possibilities.

Every next-token guess is really “how likely is each word next?”. Probability is that language.

  • A die: each face 1/6 — a distribution sums to 1.
  • Given “it is sunny”, “bring an umbrella?” is conditional.
  • An LM computes: given the context, how likely is each next token.
  • Distribution: each candidate gets a probability in [0,1]; they sum to 1.
  • Conditional probability P(next | context): given the text so far, how likely each next token is — what an LLM computes.
  • Mean is the centre; variance the spread. Attention’s √d scaling uses “variances add”.
  • Gaussian: the bell curve. Next step: with Gaussian noise, maximum likelihood recovers MSE.
  • Left: scores for 4 candidates; middle: probabilities (sum to 100%); right: the smaller the right answer’s probability, the bigger the later penalty.
  • 2.0 / 1.0 / 0.1 / −0.5 → 62.5% / 23.0% / 9.4% / 5.1%.
  • Candidates blue/green/red/sky at 62.5% / 23.0% / 9.4% / 5.1% .
  • After “the sky is”, “blue” has the highest conditional probability.
  • P(next|context) sums to 1
  • μ, variance
  • P(blue|“sky is”) + P(green|…) + … = 1
  • μ = (1/n) Σ xᵢ; variance ≈ average squared deviation
  • conditional probability: what an LM computes every step
  • mean / variance: needed later for √d scaling
