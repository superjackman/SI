Word vectors

Learn embeddings from context

▶ Open the interactive version (diagrams, labs, quizzes)

Turn discrete words into vectors — ideally vectors that carry meaning.

q = [3, 1], k = [2, 2.5]. What is the dot product?

Multiply each pair, then add: 3 × 2 + 1 × 2.5 = 8.5.

Computers need numbers, not the word “cat”. Learn a row of numbers that carries meaning; decide how big each piece is with BPE.

  • One-hot IDs are unique but say nothing about similarity.
  • Embeddings place words that share contexts nearby.
  • BPE chooses the token grain before embedding.
  • One-hot fails: with 50,000 words each is a 50,000-long vector with a single 1; any two different words have dot product 0, so “cat” is as far from “dog” as from “car”.
  • Embeddings: give each word a dense short vector (say 300 numbers), itself learned. A lookup equals one-hot × embedding matrix E.
  • word2vec (Mikolov et al., 2013): “a word is known by its context” — predict the neighbours from the centre word (skip-gram) or the reverse (CBOW); words in similar contexts end up close.
  • This is self-supervised: the labels come from the text itself. It grows into pre-training at “Pre-training”.
  • Tokenisation (BPE): start from characters or bytes, repeatedly merge the most frequent adjacent pair until the vocabulary reaches tens of thousands; decide token grain before embedding .
  • Closer directions → larger dot/cosine; cat·dog > cat·car.
  • Dot : 3 × 2 + 1 × 2.5 = 8.5. BPE : the corpus has low, lower, lowest: “l o” is most common, merge to “lo”; then “lo w” to “low”; lowest ends up as low + est.
  • Dot product : q = [3, 1], k = [2, 2.5], 3 × 2 + 1 × 2.5 = 8.5.
  • cat = [0.8, 0.1, 0.6], dog = [0.7, 0.2, 0.5], car = [0.1, 0.9, 0.0]
  • cat·dog = 0.56 + 0.02 + 0.30 = 0.88; cat·car = 0.08 + 0.09 + 0 = 0.17
  • “king − man + woman ≈ queen” is empirical and doesn’t always hold.
  • BPE : the corpus has low, lower, lowest: “l o” is most common, merge to “lo”; then “lo w” to “low”; lowest ends up as low + est.

  • P(context|centre) = softmax(u·v)
  • Dot product a · b = Σ ai × bi; cosine similarity a · b / (‖a‖ × ‖b‖)
  • Skip-gram maximises P(context | centre) = softmax(u_context · v_centre)
  • BPE: repeatedly merge the most frequent adjacent symbol pair
