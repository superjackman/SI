Word vectors

Learn embeddings from context

▶ Open the interactive version (diagrams, labs, quizzes)

Turn discrete words into vectors — ideally vectors that carry meaning.

Computers need numbers, not the word “cat”. Learn vectors that carry meaning; decide token size with BPE.

  • One-hot IDs are unique but say nothing about similarity.
  • Embeddings place words that share contexts nearby.
  • BPE chooses the token grain before embedding.
  • One-hot fails: with 50,000 words each is a 50,000-long vector with a single 1; any two different words have dot product 0, so “cat” is as far from “dog” as from “car”.
  • Embeddings: give each word a dense short vector (say 300 numbers), itself learned. A lookup equals one-hot × embedding matrix E.
  • word2vec (Mikolov et al., 2013): “a word is known by its context” — predict the neighbours from the centre word (skip-gram) or the reverse (CBOW); words in similar contexts end up close.
  • This is self-supervised: the labels come from the text itself. It grows into pre-training at step 9.
  • Tokenisation (BPE): start from characters or bytes, repeatedly merge the most frequent adjacent pair until the vocabulary reaches tens of thousands; decide token grain before embedding (K02, KC03).
  • Closer directions → larger dot/cosine; cat·dog > cat·car.
