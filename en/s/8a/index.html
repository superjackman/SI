Attention: who relates to whom

Q/K/V, √d scaling, causal mask

▶ Open the interactive version (diagrams, labs, quizzes)

The problem Let every word look straight at any other word in the sentence, with all positions computed at once (Vaswani et al., 2017, “Attention Is All You Need”).

Why care

“The cat sat on the mat as it was tired.” Who is “it”? You know at a glance: the cat. The trick that lets AI read sentences like this is attention.

Everyday analogy

  • At a party you want someone to fix your computer. You have a need in mind, everyone wears a name tag, and each has a skill.
  • The better a tag matches your need, the more you listen to that person. The advice you end up with = everyone’s skills mixed by how well they matched. Every word in the sentence asks the same: who here matters most to me?

Core idea

  • Self-attention: each word’s vector is projected into Q (what I need), K (what I offer) and V (what I hand over). Q·K gives the match, softmax turns it into weights, and the weights mix the V’s — a soft HashMap.get.
  • Divide by √d_k: if q and k have unit variance per dimension, their dot product has variance d_k; large values saturate softmax and kill the gradient. E.g. .
  • Causal mask: future positions get a score of −∞, so after softmax their weight is 0 and each word sees only itself and the words before it.
  • In training the whole sentence goes in at once; with the mask, every position’s next-token prediction comes out of one matrix multiply. That is why it parallelises.

What the picture shows

Formula

  • Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q × K^T / √d_k) × V
  • Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q × K^T / √d_k) × V
  • Causal mask: future positions (j > i) get a score of −∞
  • Q × K^T: need against name tag, one match score (dot product) for every pair of words
  • / √d_k: divide by the square root of the width, so scores do not grow so large that one word takes everything
  • softmax: turn the scores into attention shares that add up to 100%
  • × V: mix everyone’s skills by those shares; the causal mask sets the scores of “future” words to −∞, so their weight is 0

Left open One pass of attention lets each word take information from the others. But one layer finds only one kind of relation and has no step for “thinking it over”; a full model that stacks dozens of layers needs a few more parts.
