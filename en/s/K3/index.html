Math · Probability & information

Distributions → softmax → cross-entropy → KL: how a model expresses uncertainty and how it is scored.

The probability and information theory a language model uses, with step-by-step proofs of the variance identities, Bayes’ rule, softmax shift invariance, the cross-entropy decomposition and KL ≥ 0.

Why it’s designed this way: You need these to understand the loss function and the KL term in alignment training.

▶ Open the interactive version (tap the diagram, try labs, take the quiz)

🧒 In plain words

When an AI talks, it is really guessing the next word, again and again. It does not guess just one: it gives every possible word a chance (a probability). For “The weather today is so ___”, “nice” might get 70%, “hot” 20%, and all the other words share the last 10% (illustrative).

This stop covers three things: how chances work, how the model’s scores become chances (softmax), and how to score “how badly it guessed” (cross-entropy). Training means making that “badness” score smaller and smaller.
