Reasoning models + long context

Test-time compute, long windows, on-device

▶ Open the interactive version (diagrams, labs, quizzes)

The problem Spend more compute when answering, and fit longer text in one go.

Why care

Hard problems want longer thinking; long docs want wider windows.

Everyday analogy

  • Test-time compute: long CoT, voting, search.
  • Long context: bigger windows, middle still hard.
  • On-device: distill + quantise.

Core idea

  • Test-time compute: longer chains of thought, majority vote across samples, search + verifier; same line as step 10 reasoning models empirical.
  • Long context: RoPE extrapolation/interpolation, sparse attention, cache compression; “fits” ≠ “used well” (lost in the middle, Liu et al., 2023) empirical.
  • On-device small models: distillation + quantisation + efficient architectures.

What the picture shows

  • Think longer at answer time; or fit a long doc.

The numbers

  • Five samples [12,12,15,12,9] → majority 12 (KA09).
  • Five samples [12, 12, 15, 12, 9] → majority vote 12 (same as card KA09).

Formula

  • majority(samples)
  • window ↑ ≠ middle-use ↑
  • Self-consistency: majority over samples
  • Longer window ↑ does not mean middle-content use ↑
  • Same architecture as step 10’s reasoning line
