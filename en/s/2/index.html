Gradient descent

Step against the gradient, again and again

▶ Open the interactive version (diagrams, labs, quizzes)

Find the minimum of L(θ) with many parameters.

A large model has billions of parameters; you cannot try them one by one. Gradient descent is “walking downhill blindfolded”: at each step, feel which way is steepest under your feet and take a small step down. Every large model is trained this way.

  • You stand blindfolded on the inside of a big bowl and want to reach the bottom. You cannot see it, but your feet feel the slope: how steep it is east–west, and how steep north–south.
  • Put the two slopes together and you get the steepest way up. Take a small step the opposite way, feel again, and step by step you reach the bottom. Both slopes are felt at the same spot, and the step moves in both directions at once.
  • With one parameter, the derivative says “nudge the parameter up a little: how does the loss change?”. With two, the loss is a bowl L(w1, w2): the partial derivative ∂L/∂w1 is the slope when only w1 moves and w2 stays put, and ∂L/∂w2 likewise. All the partial derivatives in one vector make the gradient, pointing where the loss rises fastest.
  • Take a small step the other way and the loss falls. Repeat. Each step computes all the partial derivatives at the same point and updates every parameter at the same time; it does not finish w1 before touching w2 (that would be coordinate descent).
  • Why not solve it exactly? Linear regression has the normal equations, but they need a matrix inverse (cubic cost); a neural network’s loss is non-linear and non-convex, with no closed form. Gradient descent only needs a gradient.
  • The bowl seen from above: each ring is a contour line, smaller rings mean lower loss, and the bottom is at (2, 1).
  • From (0, 0): the yellow arrow is downhill when only w1 moves, the green arrow when only w2 moves; together they make the purple arrow, the negative gradient: one step changes both parameters. The white dots are the next few steps.
  • A partial derivative means “move one, keep the rest”: from (0, 0) set w1 to 0.001 and L goes from 6 to 5.996001; the change ÷ 0.001 ≈ −4, which is ∂L/∂w1.
