Math · Optimization

Steepest descent → learning rate → mini-batches → Adam: the math of every training step.

Proves that the negative gradient is the steepest descent direction, the learning-rate convergence condition, that mini-batch gradients are unbiased with variance falling as 1/B, and where Adam’s bias correction comes from.

Why it’s designed this way: The answers to “it diverged”, “too noisy” and “what learning rate?” are all here.

🧒 In plain words

Training a model is like walking down a hill blindfolded: you cannot see the bottom, but your feet can feel which way slopes down most steeply, so you take a small step that way, feel again, and step again. Here “how high you are” is how wrong the model is (the loss).

This stop explains why a few downhill tricks work: why you head the steepest way down; how big each step should be (the learning rate); why you can estimate the slope from a small handful of examples (mini-batches); and how remembering your last few steps makes the walk steadier (momentum and Adam).
