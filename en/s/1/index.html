Loss functions

Mean squared error, maximum likelihood, cross-entropy

▶ Open the interactive version (diagrams, labs, quizzes)

We need one number for “how wrong is the model” — smaller is better.

Every time the model guesses, it first needs to know “how wrong was that?”. Without that number it cannot tell which way to change. A loss function is the penalty for each guess, and training makes the penalty smaller and smaller.

  • Think of a weather forecaster. He says “90% rain tomorrow” and it is sunny: he should lose a lot of points. Had he said “50% rain”, he would lose fewer. The surer the claim and the more wrong it is, the bigger the penalty.
  • A model works the same way: it gives each candidate answer a score, turns the scores into probabilities, and then looks only at the probability of the right answer: the less it gave, the higher the penalty.
  • Regression (predict a number): square the errors and average them — mean squared error.
  • How the model gives odds: its last layer first gives every candidate a score (logit), positive or negative; softmax turns them into probabilities that are all positive and add up to 100% .
  • Classification (predict a class): how much probability does the model give the right answer? That is maximum likelihood; its negative log is cross-entropy.
  • Maximum likelihood under Gaussian noise gives exactly mean squared error, so MSE is not arbitrary.
  • Left: the scores the model gives four candidate tokens, positive or negative. Middle: softmax exponentiates (everything turns positive) and divides by the total, giving probabilities that add up to 100%. Right: the smaller the right answer’s probability, the bigger the penalty −ln p.
  • If the right answer is “blue”, the penalty is −ln 0.625 ≈ 0.47; the closer the probability gets to 100%, the closer the penalty gets to 0.
  • MSE: predictions 2.5, 5, 7.5 vs truth 3, 5, 7 — squared errors 0.25, 0, 0.25, mean ≈ 0.167.
  • Softmax : scores for blue / green / red / sky 2.0 / 1.0 / 0.1 / −0.5 → probabilities 62.5% / 23.0% / 9.4% / 5.1%.
  • Cross-entropy : at the start this site’s model gives the right next character only 5.4% → L = −ln 0.0536 ≈ 2.93; after 200 training steps 99.8% → L ≈ 0.002. Confidently wrong costs the most.
  • softmax(xᵢ) = e^xᵢ ÷ Σ e^xⱼ = pᵢ
  • L = −ln p(right answer)
  • MSE = (1/n) × Σ (y_pred − y_true)²
  • softmax(xᵢ) = e^xᵢ ÷ Σ e^xⱼ: exponentiate to make everything positive, then divide by the total
  • MSE = (1/n) × Σ (y_pred − y_true)²
  • negative log-likelihood = − Σ log P(yi | xi; θ) (a product underflows; logs turn it into a sum)
  • H(p, q) = − Σ p(k) × log q(k); with one-hot p it is −log q(right class)
  • KL(p‖q) = H(p, q) − H(p): with p fixed, minimising cross-entropy = minimising KL = maximum likelihood
  • xᵢ: the score (logit) the model gives candidate i
  • pᵢ: the probability after softmax; together they add up to 1
  • L: take only the right answer’s probability and its negative log; that is the cross-entropy loss
  • MSE: for predicting a number (regression), square the differences and average them

Left open With a loss L(θ), the next job is to find the θ that makes L smallest — with thousands of parameters. How?
