Math · Derivations & proofs

Backprop → p − y → √dₖ → residuals → policy gradient: full derivations of the model’s key formulas.

Applies the first four stops’ tools to the real model, deriving backprop, the output-layer gradient, attention scaling, residual gradients and the policy gradient step by step, with assumptions and sources.

Why it’s designed this way: These derivations explain why the model is designed as it is: not by whim, but to keep gradients flowing.

▶ Open the interactive version (tap the diagram, try labs, take the quiz)

🧒 In plain words

The tools from the last few stops (slopes, the chain rule, chances, tables of numbers) are used here to explain the most important formulas in real models. It is like learning to add and multiply, then doing some real sums.

Each one is proved step by step, with its assumptions spelled out. Once you follow them, you see why models are built the way they are: not by guesswork, but so that the “how to adjust” message reaches every knob.

📑 What this stop covers

  • Derive the model’s most important formulas from scratch
    The first four stops built the tools; here they are applied to the real model: Backprop derivation, the output layer’s Softmax + CE gradient p − y, attention’s Attention’s √dₖ…
  • 🔁 Backpropagation: the full derivation for two layers
    A network is an assembly line, one layer after another. Once you know how wrong the final answer is, you need to know which way, and how far, to turn every knob.
  • ⚠️ Easy to mix up: backpropagation vs gradient descent
    Backpropagation is measuring the slope: trace back from the result to work out which way, and how much, each knob should turn. Gradient descent is actually turning them a little…
  • 🧪 Backprop vs numerical gradient: check the derivation yourself (hands-on lab)
  • 🎯 Why the softmax + cross-entropy gradient is p − y
    The model scores every word, softmax turns the scores into chances, and cross-entropy scores “how badly it guessed”. The result is strikingly simple: how to adjust each score is…
  • 📏 Why attention divides by √dₖ
    In attention, each word takes its own list of numbers (the query) and multiplies it position by position with another word’s list (the key), adding everything up into a match…
  • 🛤️ Residual connections: the gradient always has a direct path
    A residual connection: each layer does not replace its input outright; it adds a small change on top: output = input + change. Like marking up a draft essay instead of rewriting…
  • 🎮 Policy gradient: a gradient from nothing but “good or bad”
    When training a model on problems, sometimes all you know is “1 point if right, 0 if wrong”, not “how far off”. A score like that has no slope on its own.
