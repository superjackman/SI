Let AI do tasks

Pick an agent by task, with you or alone, then check the result.

Say the job, choose with-you or on-its-own, then check the result yourself.

Why it’s designed this way: Things called “AI” differ a lot; the right helper saves time and mistakes.

🧒 In plain words

📏 Covered here: which kind of AI fits seven everyday jobs (code, office writing, research, spreadsheets, images and video, scheduled chores, study), which products work in mainland China, and what to watch out for. Not covered: signing up for, paying for or reaching overseas products.

Getting AI to work is like hiring a helper: say what the job is, decide whether you watch or let it work alone, then check the result yourself.

📑 What this stop covers

  • 🤖 Let AI do tasks for you: pick the right helper
    An “agent” is an AI that acts: it does not just answer, it reads files, edits code, searches the web and runs on a schedule. Different jobs suit different agents.
  • 🤝 With you vs 🚀 on its own
    With you is like a coach at your side; on its own is like handing a parcel to a courier and checking it when it arrives. When unsure, work alongside it first.
  • 🗒️ Brief it well: four lines
    Briefing is like writing a note on a food order: what you want, what to use, what to leave out, what counts as done. Four clear lines make a big difference.
  • 🛡️ Before you hand it over: three lines you do not cross
    Three lines: keep private details back, press the last irreversible button yourself, check the result yourself.
  • ⚠️ Easy to mix up: chat assistant vs agent
    A chat assistant is like a friend who hands you a recipe; an agent is like a chef who cooks. The chef does more and can also mess up the kitchen, so agree first what it may use.
  • ⚠️ Easy to mix up: looks right vs is right
    An AI answer is like a beautifully wrapped box: nice wrapping does not mean the contents are right. Open and check numbers, citations and code.
  • 🧪 Pick an agent by task: tap a job you want done (hands-on lab)
